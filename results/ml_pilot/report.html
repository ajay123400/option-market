Machine-learning pilot — does it beat the simple IV rule?

Verdict

    Out-of-sample results — every rule on the same days

    09:30 entries 1–4 trading days before expiry, held to expiry, 1 lot, all charges. Every model and every IV threshold was fitted only on data before the quarter it traded. Best ₹/trade per strategy shaded.

    Forecasting the move (to expiry ÷ straddle)

    Rank correlation between the forecast and the actual move ÷ straddle, out of sample. Higher = the forecast sorts calm and wild weeks better.

    Which inputs helped the boosted model (out of sample)

    Permutation importance on the next quarter: how much the forecast got worse when that input was shuffled. Negative = the model did better without it, i.e. it learned noise.

    Setup

    • Inputs at 09:30: ATM IV, straddle % of NIFTY, IV rank, term structure, 25Δ/10Δ skew, smile curvature, days to expiry, weekday, realized vol 5/20 days, yesterday's range and move, today's gap, the 09:15–09:30 range and move, IV ÷ realized.
    • Vol model: gradient boosting on log(|NIFTY move to expiry| ÷ straddle); sell when the forecast is below ${CUT}. P&L model: gradient boosting on the strategy's ₹ result; sell when the forecast is above 0.
    • Walk-forward: trained on Sep 2021 onwards, first used Oct 2022, re-trained every quarter; rows whose expiry had not happened yet were never used for training.
    • Not yet included: India VIX, event calendar (RBI, budget, results, US data), global overnight markets. They could help the move forecast; the pilot says the base is weak, so they would need to add a lot.
