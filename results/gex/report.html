Does dealer gamma exposure (GEX) help an option seller?

What the data says

    IV-rule sell days, split by GEX sign

    Straddle to expiry = ATM straddle sold at the 09:31 close − 0.5 pt, held to the expiry close, 1 lot, after charges (setup A style).

    All days, by GEX sign within IV terciles

    Range ratio = rest-of-day NIFTY high−low ÷ the one-day move ATM IV implied. Theory says negative-GEX days should be wilder (higher ratio).

    All days, by GEX tilt quintile (net ÷ total gamma)

    Method & limits

    • 09:30 snapshot of the nearest weekly expiry (0–4 trading days left), strikes within ±8% of NIFTY. Gamma from Black-76 on the put-call-parity forward, IV from the out-of-the-money option at each strike; OI at 09:30.
    • Net GEX assumes dealers are long the calls and short the puts that customers trade (the usual convention). Who is really short in NIFTY options is not known from OI; the tilt quintiles and the total-gamma check cover the other readings.
    • Only the nearest expiry is counted (the next week's contracts are not in the 1-min files on most days). Total gamma mostly tracks days-to-expiry, so it is not used as a separate signal.
