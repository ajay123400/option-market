What this says

  • The Day-1 range holds the expiry close about 4 times in 10 (40.9%). Misses are evenly split between above and below, so the range has no directional bias.
  • The leader range is no better than a plain band. A band of the same width centred on NIFTY at 09:20 held the expiry close 44.0% of the time, as often or more. The volume leaders mainly tell you how wide the market expects the move to be (the range width tracks the straddle premium), not where it will expire.
  • The range is almost always tested: NIFTY trades outside the Day-1 range at some point in 95.4% of weeks. A positional version must expect adjustments; a "set and forget" range will be crossed.
  • Selling the Day-1 range strikes and holding to expiry wins about 2 times in 3 (66.1%, median +82.9 pts) but the losers are large (worst -707.0 pts) — the classic short-option profile. Over 5 years it is only mildly positive and two years were negative, so the adjustment and stop rules are what decide the result.
  • Wider Day-1 ranges did better (see width table): the widest half expired inside ~50% of the time and was profitable to hold; the narrowest half ~32% and lost. Range width is a useful filter to research further.
